On a Plane ✈

Communicate confidently from boarding to landing — find your seat, make polite requests, report a problem, and understand what the cabin crew tell you. The core skill: listening for gist — catching the main idea without getting lost in every small detail.

Beyond the Plane

Why gist listening matters everywhere

🎯 The gist skill you build here transfers directly to the news, lectures, company meetings, and long stories. Once you can catch the main idea of a cabin announcement without panicking about every word, you can do the same when a colleague explains a project or a professor gives a talk. Understanding the big picture first, details second — that is the habit this lesson trains.

Part 1CConnected Speech

Could you → "couldja"

Native speakers blur polite requests together. You don't have to copy it — but you must be able to recognize it when you hear it.

🔗 Think of it like joining train carriages. "Could" and "you" are two carriages; in fast speech they couple together into one: couldja. The words don't change meaning — they just connect. This is called connected speech, and airline crew use it constantly.
could you→"couldja"
/ˈkʊdʒə/ — the d + y melt into a "j" sound
would you→"wouldja"
/ˈwʊdʒə/ — same rule: d + y → "j"

Part 3Listening Strategy

Listening for Gist

Before you listen for details, train the most important skill: catching the main idea.

🎯 What is "gist"?

Gist = the main idea. When you listen for gist, you are NOT trying to catch every word. You ask three big questions: Who is talking? What is the topic/problem? How do they feel? Everything else is detail — you can let it go.

🧳 Think of it like packing a suitcase. You pack the big items first — the main idea — then fit small things around them. If you try to grab every sock before the jacket, you run out of space and panic. Catch the big idea first; the details settle in around it.
